# Morning Market Brief — Commodities
**Thursday, July 30, 2026**

## 1. Macro Backdrop

Risk sentiment across energy, metals, and ags remains hostage to the Strait of Hormuz, where the US and Iran are now five months into a conflict that briefly de-escalated last month on a ceasefire memorandum before fighting resumed this week. Large-vessel traffic through the US-coordinated Oman-hugging lane has effectively halted since Tuesday, and the two sides are trading missile barrages with no resolution in sight — this is the single biggest swing factor for the trading day ahead across every commodities complex.

On the policy side, the Fed held rates at Wednesday's FOMC meeting, but three members dissented in favor of a hike and Chair Kevin Warsh flagged that persistent inflation could force the Committee's hand. The dollar whipsawed lower on the decision before recovering into this morning, and Treasury yields remain elevated. US Q2 GDP growth also came in soft at 1.5%, missing forecasts — a stagflation-adjacent mix (soft growth, sticky inflation risk, hawkish-leaning Fed) that's keeping cross-asset volatility elevated into the open.

## 2. Oil

- **WTI:** ~$84.59/bbl at the open
- **Brent:** opened ~$90.46/bbl, spiked intraday to $92.65 — now roughly $4/bbl above last week's level even after briefly round-tripping back to pre-war prices last month

Hormuz remains the story: no large tankers have crossed the US-monitored route since Tuesday, and Iran's control of the strait raises the risk it reimposes the transit tolls it levied earlier in the war. Layered on top:
- A drone strike near the Suez Canal in Egypt has opened a second security front for tanker routing, with a Saudi-led maritime defense coalition now being floated as a response.
- Washington sanctioned support networks for Iran's Mahan Air, tightening the screws further.
- Russia extended its diesel and gasoline export bans into 2027 and is turning to Kazakhstan for refining capacity, while record US refinery runs are still failing to close the global fuel crunch — bullish for cracks even independent of the Hormuz risk premium.

Net: the market is pricing a "start-stop" war premium, but desks should watch for headline-driven mean reversion if a de-escalation signal hits — the CNN framing this week was literally "oil keeps tumbling even when the Iran war drags on," a reminder this tape is two-sided.

## 3. Natural Gas

- **Henry Hub:** ~$2.90–$3.29/MMBtu — stable, ample US production and only moderate cooling demand are keeping the domestic market well-balanced, a sharp contrast to the international benchmarks below.
- **TTF (Europe):** ~$19.2/MMBtu, elevated on the same geopolitical risk complex.
- **JKM (Asia):** pushed into the high-$20s/MMBtu as Northeast Asian buyers price in Hormuz-linked LNG supply disruption risk.

The US/international divergence is the trade here: domestic gas is shrugging off the same headlines moving Brent and JKM double digits, which keeps US LNG export economics attractive but also means the domestic complex is one of the few "quiet" corners of the commodities tape this morning.

## 4. Metals

- **Gold:** ~$4,080–$4,091/oz, up ~0.4% — sitting in record territory, supported by both the Fed's hold and safe-haven flow out of the Hormuz conflict.
- **Silver:** ~$58/oz, a touch softer overnight but still historically elevated.
- **Copper:** $6.44/lb, +2.5% — tightening supply is doing the work here, distinct from the safe-haven bid in precious metals.

**FX read-through:** the dollar's post-FOMC round trip (sharp drop, partial recovery) is the swing factor for precious metals into the session — Warsh's hawkish tone is dollar-supportive on the margin, which is the main headwind risk to gold's rally if it persists through the day. Watch DXY alongside gold rather than trading gold in isolation this morning.

## 5. Ags

**G&Os:**
- **Wheat:** ~$6.80–$7.00+/bu, up sharply (+1.8% just yesterday) and closing in on the two-year high near $7 set July 22 — intensifying Russia-Ukraine hostilities are the driver, threatening Black Sea export flows from two of the world's top wheat producers. French soft wheat yields were also just cut ~3% below average, adding a supply-side leg to the rally.
- **Soybeans:** August contract at $11.78, -34c on the day — a give-back after a +9.2% run driven by higher fertilizer costs and strong Chinese demand; watch whether this is profit-taking or a genuine turn.
- **Corn:** September contract $4.49, -9.5c — softer, taking a cue from the broader grain pullback.

**Softs:**
- **Coffee:** -15%, now at its lowest level since September 2024, on rising Vietnamese exports and expectations for a record Brazilian harvest.
- **Sugar:** at its lowest since October 2020 on surplus Brazilian rainfall.

Ags is bifurcated this morning: wheat is the geopolitical-risk trade, while coffee and sugar are pure supply-glut stories moving in the opposite direction.

## 6. Key Themes to Watch

- **Hormuz trajectory:** any de-escalation or fresh escalation headline (tolls, ceasefire talk, further strikes) will whip oil, gas, and gold simultaneously — this is the dominant risk factor today.
- **Suez Canal security:** follow-through from the Egypt drone strike and the proposed Saudi-led maritime coalition.
- **Fed communication:** parse any further commentary from Warsh or other FOMC members after Wednesday's 3-dissent hold.
- **PCE inflation:** the next print (covering July) isn't due until August 26, but June's report (released this week) showed consumer spending +0.3% and cooling inflation — a data point the market will keep referencing until the next release.
- **Russia-Ukraine grain corridor risk:** the direct driver of the wheat rally; any escalation there extends the move.
- **China–Central Asia trade:** China's trade with Central Asia surged 6.5%, worth watching as a China-demand signal for base metals and ags.

## 7. P&L Impact & Trade Ideas

- **Energy:** tactical long bias on the war-risk premium remains defensible, but size for two-way volatility — headline-driven mean reversion is a real risk if any Hormuz or Suez de-escalation signal lands. Refined products (diesel/gasoline) look structurally tight into 2027 on the Russian export bans — cracks may be the cleaner expression of the fuel-crunch thesis versus outright crude.
- **Gold:** safe-haven long remains intact, but hedge the dollar leg — a sustained hawkish Fed tone is the main risk to the rally continuing at this pace.
- **Copper:** supply-tightness-driven strength looks more durable than the geopolitical bid elsewhere in metals; less binary risk than gold or oil into today's session.
- **Wheat:** carry the long into the Russia-Ukraine headlines; French yield cuts add a second supply leg independent of the war risk.
- **Soybeans/Corn:** yesterday's pullback is worth fading only if Chinese demand data confirms the underlying strength story remains intact — otherwise treat as a legitimate turn.
- **Coffee/Sugar:** structurally bearish on supply glut; not a "buy the dip" tape yet given record harvest expectations.
