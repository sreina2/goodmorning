**Friday, July 31, 2026**

**Macro backdrop.** Second FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh, same result as the first: hold, but the vote split (9-3) and the market reaction are the story. The 30-year sold off through the decision to 5.21% — the highest since 2007 — as the bond market effectively challenged Warsh's inflation credibility in real time. That's the dominant cross-asset theme into today: a Fed that held but didn't convince. In Europe, the ECB held at a 2.25% deposit rate after June's hike, and the BoE held Bank Rate at 4.00% with Bailey explicitly pushing back on hike pricing. Verified: all three central bank decisions. Best-guess: how durable Warsh's credibility problem proves through the next data cycle is the open question desks are positioning around.

**Rates.** Front end: 2yr UST ~4.24%, down ~4bp post-FOMC — short end reading the hold as dovish-enough near-term. Belly-to-long end: 10yr ~4.66%, 30yr ~5.21% — a bear-steepener, with the long end doing the work on the inflation-credibility repricing rather than the front end pricing hikes. In Europe, 2yr gilts fell 11bp to 4.34% on Bailey's pushback, 10yr gilt ~4.35%; BoE QT pace is expected to slow (gilt runoff from £70bn toward £50bn annually — best-guess, not yet confirmed in the MPC minutes). Bund yields have been choppy on the same geopolitical inflation channel, with markets now pricing three ECB hikes in 2026, up from two — a live repricing worth tracking, flagged as directionally consistent across sources but not a single verified number.

**FX.** DXY around 100.9, off its early-July highs near 101.4. EUR/USD ~1.14, holding. USD/JPY ~160.5, having recovered off a two-month low near 158 — yen weakness is structural here, not a one-day move. EM: local-currency debt is the preferred expression through H2 given real yield support and the dollar's broader downward bias if that view holds — CEEMEA screens as the highest-yield opportunity set, Asia is getting an AI-trade-cycle tailwind, LatAm has resilient domestic demand plus easing cycles underway. EM debt took a brief hit when the Iran conflict first broke and the dollar spiked, but has largely round-tripped that move over the past quarter.

**Key themes and data today.** Watch: (1) any follow-through commentary from Warsh or other FOMC members defending or walking back Wednesday's tone — this is the single biggest swing factor for rates today; (2) BoE/ECB speaker commentary reinforcing or pushing back on the hawkish repricing; (3) the ongoing Iran conflict as the macro wildcard feeding directly into both the long-end inflation premium and EM/FX risk sentiment; (4) desks should also watch for any read-through from Thursday's Fed reaction into today's UST auction demand, given the credibility question is now a live auction-tail risk, not just a talking point.

**Positioning and trade ideas.** The trade here is the curve, not the level — a bear-steepener bias (long front end / short long end, or outright long-end underweight) is the cleanest way to express the Warsh-credibility story without taking a hard directional rate call, and it's consistent with what the tape actually did into the decision; on FX, fade dollar strength into EM local-currency carry given the H2 flow backdrop, but keep it sized for Hormuz-driven risk-off spikes given how quickly the dollar and EM both re-priced when the conflict first broke; in G10, the BoE's dovish pushback versus the ECB's more hawkish drift argues for a tactical long GBP/EUR expression, though this is a best-guess overlay on top of verified policy divergence rather than a confirmed flow story.
