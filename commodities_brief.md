*Friday, July 31, 2026*

**Macro backdrop.** Continuing the theme from Wednesday's FOMC (held, 3 dissents, Chair Warsh flagging inflation risk), the last 48 hours have been dominated by a whipsaw Hormuz narrative: a mid-week pause let tanker traffic recover to an estimated 30–35% of pre-war levels (Commonwealth Bank estimate) and briefly cut Fed hike odds from 37.4% to 30.5% as inflation risk eased — only for Iran's Revolutionary Guard to reportedly strike two tankers transiting the Strait on Friday, reversing the de-escalation trade in real time. Layer on President Trump's push to attach new Iran tariffs to a bipartisan Russia/Iran sanctions bill, and the macro backdrop into today is genuinely two-sided: every commodity below is trading off the same on-again, off-again conflict premium.

**Oil.** WTI +2.2% to ~\$85.41/bbl and Brent +1.5% to ~\$90.36/bbl on Friday's tanker-strike headlines, clawing back part of the week's de-escalation selloff — WTI had held near \$84 earlier in the week after swinging in an \$8+ range, and is still up roughly a fifth for the month. Verified: the IRGC's claimed tanker strikes and the mid-week Hormuz traffic-recovery estimate. Best-guess: how much of this bounce holds once today's headline cycle cools, given how quickly this same market round-tripped the last de-escalation move.

**Natural gas.** Henry Hub eased to ~\$2.70/MMBtu (-3.9%), TTF fell to ~€58.54/MWh (-6.5%), and JKM slipped to ~\$21.33/MMBtu (-2.0%) as the mid-week Hormuz pause unwound the LNG risk premium across Europe and Asia. Best-guess: Friday's fresh tanker-strike headlines likely put a floor back under TTF/JKM into today's session, though same-day confirmation on that repricing isn't in yet — Henry Hub should stay comparatively insulated given ample domestic supply.

**Metals.** Gold pulled back to ~\$4,040/oz from Thursday's ~\$4,071 as the safe-haven bid cooled alongside the Hormuz pause, while silver whipsawed — popping above \$59 on the pause-driven risk-on move before fading back to ~\$57.94 intraday. Copper continues to grind higher, +0.14% to \$6.45/lb, a steadier tightening-supply story holding up independent of the Hormuz on/off cycle. FX tie-in: the dollar itself whipsawed this week (a two-day surge, then a slip on Mideast developments, then a further pre-Fed drift lower) — treat DXY as the fastest-moving read on which way the Hormuz story is breaking at any given moment, since gold and silver are largely trading as its mirror image.

**Ags.** Wheat continues its slow grind higher (WEAT +0.49%, September Chicago wheat +2.75c to \$6.635) on the same Black Sea/geopolitical risk premium that's been building all week. Soybeans and corn are both marginally softer (SOYB -0.12%, CORN -0.39%; August soybeans -0.75c to \$11.77, September corn -3.25c to \$4.4575) as supportive Midwest-weather and revived-Chinese-demand narratives offset each other rather than driving a clean directional move. Coffee and sugar remain near multi-year lows (coffee -15% from recent highs on Vietnamese export strength and a record Brazilian harvest outlook; sugar at its lowest since October 2020 on Brazilian rainfall surplus) — both are pure supply-glut stories, decoupled from the geopolitical premium moving everything else.

**Key themes and data today.** Watch: (1) whether Friday's IRGC tanker-strike claims are confirmed or escalate further — the single biggest swing factor for oil, gas, and gold simultaneously; (2) any follow-through on Trump's proposed Iran-tariff addition to the Russia/Iran sanctions bill, which could reset the geopolitical risk premium independent of Hormuz shipping data; (3) further commentary from Warsh or other FOMC members defending Wednesday's hold, given hike odds were already swinging (37.4% to 30.5%) purely on Hormuz-driven inflation repricing before the decision even landed; (4) confirmation — or not — of the Commonwealth Bank's 30–35% Hormuz-traffic-recovery estimate, which is currently best-guess rather than verified.

**Positioning and trade ideas.** The Hormuz on/off cycle argues for sizing oil, gas, and gold for two-way volatility rather than a hard directional call — this week alone has round-tripped once already. Copper is the cleaner single-direction expression right now, since its supply-tightness bid isn't obviously tied to the conflict headline cycle. In ags, the wheat long is the more durable geopolitical-risk carry; soybeans and corn look more like a wait-and-see on Chinese demand data before sizing either direction. On FX, don't trade gold or silver in isolation — DXY's whipsaw is doing most of the work on precious metals moves this week, so hedge or size the dollar leg explicitly rather than treating the metals move as a standalone signal.
